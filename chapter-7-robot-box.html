💡 The big rule of this chapter The hardware is fixed. The behavior is software. Change the code, and the same box becomes a different gadget.
💡 Parent tip Steps 1 to 4 need you: they use Terminal and a new computer. You can also try the whole thing on the MacBook first, with no new parts at all. See "Try it on the Mac first" below.
⚠️ This page hasn't met a real Raspberry Pi yet The games were tested on a MacBook. The Raspberry Pi steps were written from the Pi's instructions but not run on a real one. Expect to fix a small thing or two on your first build. The "What we tested" card near the bottom has the full list.

💡 The big idea: the robot moves out of the laptop

A Raspberry Pi is a whole computer the size of a deck of cards. It runs Python, and it has a camera port, a screen port, Wi-Fi and USB.

EyesBrain (runs the AI)Screen and sound
Chapters 3 and 4📷 MacBook camera💻 MacBook🤖 Arduino, on USB
Chapter 7, Path A📷 Pi camera🍓 Raspberry Pi🤖 Arduino, on USB
Chapter 7, Path B📷 Pi camera🍓 Raspberry Pi📱 touchscreen, 🔊 speaker, 🔘 buttons, all in one box

The Pi is slower than the MacBook. The trick to making it feel just as fast is the AI Camera: it has its own little AI chip, so it can find people and body poses by itself and hand the Pi the answer.

Who does the seeing?

The AI Camera's chip can find people, pets, things and body poses. It can not find hands or faces. So the work is split:

What the app needsWho runs it
People, pets and everyday things🎥 The AI Camera's chip
Body pose (a stick figure)🎥 The AI Camera's chip
Hand gestures (Rock Paper Scissors)🍓 The Pi's processor
Faces🍓 The Pi's processor

An app never picks a model. It just says what it needs, like needs = {"hands"}, and a part of the code called the vision router decides who does the job. On the MacBook, the Mac does all of it.

🔒 Play it here: the sealed envelope

In Chapter 3 you made the robot cheat by peeking at your move. This time the robot can't cheat: it locks its move in a sealed envelope before the countdown. Try it.

The robot is choosing...
✉️🔒
You 0 · Robot 0

Log: nothing yet.

Look at the log after each round. In fair mode the robot's move is written down before yours. In cheat mode the envelope shows ?, because it's empty: the robot waits to see your move. The real box works the same way, and it has a test that checks the order.

Grown-ups call this commit and reveal. It's how you prove you didn't change your answer.

💻 Try it on the Mac first (no new parts)

🛒 Shopping list

Prices were checked in October 2026. Raspberry Pi prices rose three times in 2026 because of a worldwide memory shortage, so check before you buy. Budget: Path A roughly $230 to $250, Path B roughly $300 to $350.

PartABNotes
Raspberry Pi 5, 8 GB✅✅$130 list price. Seeing and speaking at the same time needs the room. The 4 GB ($85) works for one light app.
Official 27 W USB-C power supply✅✅Required. Weak power is the top cause of random Pi crashes. Don't use a phone charger.
Active Cooler (the Pi 5's fan)✅✅Required. AI keeps the chip busy. Without the fan, the Pi quietly slows itself down when hot.
microSD card, 32 to 64 GB✅✅A good brand, rated A2. Cheap cards wear out, and that's the most common way a Pi "dies".
Raspberry Pi AI Camera✅✅$70 list price. A normal camera for every app, plus its own AI chip. The Camera Module 3 is cheaper but has no AI chip.
Touch Display 2✅5-inch, $40 (or 7-inch, $60). Plugs into the Pi's display port.
Small USB speaker✅The Pi 5 has no headphone jack.
2 arcade buttons + jumper wires✅Big, clicky buttons are half the fun. One green, one red.
Project box✅Room for the Pi, fan, screen and speaker.
Your Chapter 1 Arduino✅optionalPath A uses it as the screen and the buzzer.
Later: NVMe SSD + M.2 HAT+optionalFaster and tougher than an SD card. Not for day one.

🅰️ Path A: start simple

🍓
Path A · Step 1

Set up the Pi

⏱ About 30 minutes · a parent job
  1. On the Mac, open Raspberry Pi Imager and write Raspberry Pi OS (64-bit) to the microSD card. In its settings: set the hostname to robotbox, pick a username, add the home Wi-Fi, and turn on SSH.
  2. With the power off: attach the cooler and the camera's ribbon cable, and push in the card.
  3. Plug in the official power supply. Wait about a minute.
  4. From the Mac's Terminal, connect to the box. Use the username you picked. 
    ssh YOUR-USERNAME@robotbox.local

🏆 The win: the Terminal line now ends in @robotbox. You're typing on the box, from the Mac.

🏅 Badge: Pi Pilot

📷
Path A · Step 2

Camera check

⏱ About 10 minutes

Take a test photo on the Pi:

rpicam-still -o test.jpg

With a monitor plugged in, rpicam-hello --timeout 5000 shows a 5-second live picture instead.

If you have the AI Camera, install its models once. The first start after this can take several minutes while the camera loads them.

sudo apt install imx500-all

🏆 The win: a file called test.jpg on the Pi, and no error message.

🏅 Badge: Eye Doctor

🧠
Path A · Step 3

Move the brain

⏱ About 30 minutes · most of it is waiting
  1. Plug your Chapter 1 Arduino into the Pi's USB port. It should still have robot_brain.ino from Chapter 3 on it.
  2. Copy the project onto the Pi. 
    git clone https://github.com/keep-code-simple/build-a-bot.git
cd build-a-bot/robot-box
  3. Run the setup script. It installs the AI, downloads the hand model, and sets up autostart for Path A. Go get a snack. 
    bash setup/install.sh --path-a
  4. Play a round. 
    .venv/bin/python box.py --app rps --arduino

Show a fist, an open hand or a peace sign to the Pi's camera. The Arduino's screen plays the round, exactly like Chapter 3. Press Control + C to stop.

🏆 The win: Rock Paper Scissors works, and the MacBook isn't doing any of the thinking.

🏅 Badge: Brain Transplant. The AI moved from the laptop into its own little computer.

💡 In Path A the Arduino still picks the robot's move That's Chapter 3's sketch, and it picks after it gets your move. The sealed envelope arrives in Path B, when the box has its own screen.

🅱️ Path B: the full box

📱
Path B · Step 1

Screen and sound

⏱ About 30 minutes
  1. Shut the Pi down and unplug it. Never connect ribbon cables with the power on.
  2. Connect the Touch Display 2: the ribbon cable to the Pi's display port, and its power wires to the GPIO pins. Follow the picture in the display's own instructions.
  3. Plug in the USB speaker.
  4. Power on, connect from the Mac, and switch the box to Path B: 
    cd build-a-bot/robot-box
bash setup/install.sh
sudo reboot

🏆 The win: after the restart, the Robot Box menu appears on the touchscreen by itself. Tap a game.

🏅 Badge: Lights, Screen, Action

🔘
Path B · Step 2

Arcade buttons

⏱ About 20 minutes · power off first

Each button has two legs. One leg goes to a GPIO pin, the other to any GND pin. No resistors needed: the software switches on the Pi's built-in ones.

ButtonPinJob
A (green)GPIO 17Select / play
B (red)GPIO 27Back / menu. Hold 2 seconds for the debug numbers.

🏆 The win: in the menu, B moves the white outline to the next app, and A opens it.

🏅 Badge: Button Masher

⚠️ Turning the box off Use the Pi 5's own little power button, and make it reachable from outside the box. Pulling the plug while the Pi is saving something can scramble the SD card.
📦
Path B · Step 3

Build the box

⏱ 60 to 90 minutes · a parent helps with cutting and drilling
  • The Pi sits on standoffs, so nothing metal touches its underside.
  • The screen looks out through a window in the front.
  • The camera goes at the top, facing out, at face height when the box sits on a table.
  • The speaker sits behind a few drilled holes.
  • The buttons go on the front. The power cable comes out the back.
  • Leave air holes near the fan. A box with no air is an oven.

Kid job: design the face of the box. Stickers, a name, a paint job. It's yours.

🏆 The win: one box, one cable, and everything still works when you close the lid.

🏅 Badge: Box Builder

✌️
Path B · Step 4

Play (forever)

⏱ As long as you like

The box boots straight into the menu. Pick Rock Paper Scissors.

  1. Show your hand to wake it up.
  2. Pick a player. Tap a name, or press A to change it. Then show your hand to start.
  3. The robot commits first. It locks its move in the sealed envelope 🔒 before the countdown.
  4. ROCK... PAPER... SCISSORS... SHOOT! Throw your move on SHOOT.
  5. The box reads your hand for half a second and takes a vote: the move it saw in most of the pictures wins. If it's not sure, it says "I couldn't see that. Again!" and the envelope stays sealed.
  6. The envelope opens. First to 3 wins the match.
  7. Leaderboard: the longest winning streak for each player is saved on the box.

🏆 The win: a fair fight against your own robot, in its own body.

🏅 Badge: It's Alive

🛠️ Make a new app (the part you come back to)

Bored of Rock Paper Scissors? Don't put the box away. Give it a new brain. Every app is just a folder.

  1. On the Mac, copy the template folder. Call the copy anything without spaces. 
    cd ~/"Claude Projects/Door Greeter Bot/robot-box"
cp -r apps/_template apps/my_app
  2. Open apps/my_app/app.py and change the name, the color, and which AI it needs.
  3. Fill in update() and draw(). That's your whole game.
  4. Try it on the Mac. 
    python box.py --desktop --app my_app
  5. Send it to the box. Push it to GitHub from the Mac. On the box, pick Check for updates in the menu, then restart.

The shape of every app

from core import draw


class App:
    name = "My App"                 # what the menu tile says
    color = (255, 170, 0)           # the tile's color: red, green, blue
    needs = set()                   # which AI to switch on, like {"hands"}

    def start(self, box):           # the app opens
        self.box = box
        self.count = 0

    def update(self, box, frame, vision, dt):   # every frame: change the game
        pass

    def draw(self, screen):         # every frame: draw the game
        wide, high = screen.get_size()
        screen.fill(draw.BLACK)
        draw.text(screen, f"You pressed A {self.count} times", (wide / 2, high / 2), 8)

    def on_button(self, box, name): # "A", "B", or ("touch", x, y)
        if name == "A":
            self.count += 1
            box.beep(880, 100)
            return True
        return False                # False for "B" means: go back to the menu

    def stop(self, box):            # the app closes
        pass

What needs can be

Ask forYou get, every frame
"hands"vision.hands: each hand's gesture name, how sure the AI is, and its 21 points
"faces"vision.faces: a box around each face
"people"vision.people: a box around each person
"objects"vision.objects: everyday things with names, like "cat" or "cup"
"pose"vision.poses: body points for a stick figure

Rock Paper Scissors needs only the hand model, and that's the one the setup downloaded. For the others, run python setup/download_models.py faces (or people, objects, pose) first.

What box can do for you

box.say("Hello!")says it out loud
box.beep(880, 200)beeps: pitch, then milliseconds
box.tune("WIN")plays a tune: HELLO, WIN, LOSE, TIE, COUNT, SHOOT or OOPS

box.scores.table("my_app")gives you the leaderboard
box.arduino.send("F")sends a Chapter 3 letter to the Arduino, if one is plugged in
box.confighands you the settings from config.json
💡 Your first app: read the Reaction Tester apps/reaction/app.py is the smallest complete game on the box, and it uses no AI. Read it top to bottom before you write your own. Then pick an idea from robot-box-ideas.md: a staring-contest judge, a pet detector, a scoreboard, and more.
💡 If your app breaks, the box doesn't A typo makes the box skip your app, and the log says why. A crash while playing shows "Oops! This app crashed" with the reason, then goes back to the menu. Breaking things is how you learn, so the box is built for it.

⚡ Fast and solid

A Pi can't match the MacBook's raw speed. It gets close by not doing work it doesn't have to.

  • Only run what's needed. An app's AI is loaded when it opens and let go when it closes.
  • Small pictures for the AI. The AI gets a shrunken copy of each picture. The screen shows the full one.
  • The camera's chip helps. People, things and poses are found by the AI Camera itself.
  • Talking never freezes the game. Sentences wait in a line, and a helper says them one by one.
  • The box watches itself. If you see "I'm too hot!" or "I need a better charger!" in the corner, believe it.
  • A crash recovers. A broken app goes back to the menu. If the whole launcher crashes, it restarts itself.

Measure, don't guess

🕵️ Troubleshooting

Detective time. Tap a problem to see what to check.

"Oops! This app crashed" says an AI model is missing

The app needs a model you haven't downloaded. The message tells you the exact command, like python setup/download_models.py hands.

My new app isn't in the menu

Check three things: the folder is inside apps/, the file is called app.py, and the folder name doesn't start with _. Then look at the Terminal (or data/box.log) for a line starting with "Skipped the app". It says what's wrong.

It says "I couldn't see that" a lot

Hold your move still on SHOOT, about an arm's length from the camera, with light on your hand and not behind it. Press D (or hold B) and check the pictures-a-second number. If it's below about 5, the box has too few pictures to vote with.

"Can't open the camera" on the Mac

Another program is using it, or Terminal isn't allowed to. Check System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera. If your iPhone's camera opens instead, change "number": 0 to 1 in config.json.

No sound on the box

Is the USB speaker plugged in? The Pi 5 has no headphone jack. Test it on the Pi with espeak-ng hello.

"I need a better charger!" or "I'm too hot!"

Charger: use the official 27 W supply, not a phone charger. Hot: is the fan spinning, and does the box have air holes near it?

The box doesn't start by itself

Connect from the Mac and look at robot-box/data/last-run.log and data/box.log. For Path A, systemctl status robot-box shows what happened.

"No Arduino found" in Path A

Is the USB cable in the Pi? Is anything else (like the Arduino app's Serial Monitor) talking to it? Only one program can talk to the Arduino at a time.

🔐 Privacy rules

  • No photos are saved. Each picture is looked at and thrown away.
  • Nothing goes to the internet. The AI runs on the box. "Check for updates" only downloads code.
  • Real names stay home. Names go in config.json, which never goes to GitHub. The shared files say "Kid 1" and "Kid 2".
  • Future apps that save pictures or know faces must say so on screen, and be switched on by you.
