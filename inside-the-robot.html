🔬 The big idea

INPUT → BRAIN → OUTPUT

Everything a robot does is signals going in, a decision, and signals going out. Each level below zooms in one step closer, all the way down to switches too small to see.

Level 1

The robot loop

Sense → think → act, over and over

Every robot does three things, again and again: notice something, decide, do something.

if (cm < 60 && !someoneHere)

Green = true, grey = false. The robot says hello only when both are green.

Now swap the input with the three tabs. Watch closely: THINK and ACT stay exactly the same. The brain doesn't care where a signal came from. A sensor, a remote and an AI are all just inputs.

🦉 The loop() function runs again and again. With delay(100) in the greeter, it runs about 10 times a second. Without the delay, it would loop thousands of times a second.

Follow the power

A brain without power is asleep. Power comes in through one of two doors.

Pick a power door.

🦉 The UNO runs on 5 V. The round jack likes 7 to 12 V, so the kit's 9 V battery fits. The "calmer" is a voltage regulator.

Pins: the brain's doors

A pin is a door. For an output, the brain opens the door and pushes electricity out. For an input, the brain listens at the door: did something arrive?

digitalWrite(13, LOW);

🦉 HIGH means about 5 V and LOW means 0 V. That's how all the robot's 1s and 0s look in real life. A pin can safely give only a small amount of current (about 20 mA is the safe everyday limit, 40 mA is the absolute maximum), roughly enough for an LED. That's why bigger things like motors need a helper: a driver chip or a transistor.

    Level 4

    Sand that thinks

    Silicon, and switches that decide

    Three kinds of stuff

    This circuit has a gap. Drag (or tap) a material to fill it and see if the light comes on.

    Pick a material.

    🦉 Pure silicon is made from sand (sand is mostly silicon and oxygen). Adding a tiny pinch of other atoms, like phosphorus or boron, is called doping. It's what makes silicon controllable.

    The transistor: a switch that electricity controls

    A transistor is a switch with no fingers. Instead of you flipping it, a little bit of electricity flips it. Press and hold the tap.

    On = 1, off = 0. That's the whole language of computers.

    🦉 The three parts are called source, drain and gate. Voltage on the gate opens a path between source and drain.

    Switches make decisions

    Now wire two switches to one lamp. Flip the switches, then change how they're connected.

    if (cm < 60 && !someoneHere)

    In a row: the lamp needs both switches on. That's AND, written &&.

    And one more: NOT. It flips a signal upside down. In code it's written !
    so !someoneHere is true

    The && in YOUR greeter code is exactly this. Billions of these tiny decisions, stacked together, make a brain.

    🖐 Try it for real Open the greeter code (Chapter 1, Step 4) and find every && and !. Each one is a little AND or NOT.
    🏅 To earn Sand Wizard: light the "Say hello!" lamp…
    • with the switches in a row (AND)
    • with the switches side by side (OR)
